## What should you look at when you compare IPTV providers?

Think of IPTV comparison like comparing cars.

You would not choose a car because the brochure says it has 300 features. You would ask whether it fits your family, your budget, your commute, and whether the thing actually works when you turn the key.

The same idea applies here.

**Price structure** matters because a cheap monthly plan can become much more expensive over a year.

**Simultaneous connections** matter when two people want to watch different screens at the same time.

**Device support** matters because your setup may be a Firestick today and a Smart TV tomorrow.

**Trial and refund terms** matter because streaming quality depends partly on your own internet connection and device.

**Channel availability** matters more than the headline channel count. A provider advertising thousands of channels is not especially useful when the five channels you care about are missing.

TiviLive currently lists one- through five-connection options and says that a connection represents one simultaneous stream. Its FAQ explains that a two-connection plan allows two screens to stream at the same time.

## How does TiviLive compare with other IPTV-style services?

This is where things get a little more interesting.

Not every IPTV provider follows the same business model, so a simple “price per channel” calculation can be misleading.

For example, Sling currently lists plans starting at $19.99 per month for Sling Essentials and offers a defined set of live-TV channels and add-ons.

Hulu + Live TV is a different kind of bundle again. Its current page advertises 100+ live channels, and its regular price for the ad-supported Hulu + Live TV plan is $99.99/month, although a limited promotion is currently showing $49.99 for the first month before returning to $99.99.

TiviLive, by comparison, advertises 28,000+ live channels and prices starting at $15/month for one connection.

Those numbers should not be read as an apples-to-apples quality comparison. Channel licensing, regional availability, local channels, add-ons, DVR features, device limits and content rights can be very different between services.

So when you compare IPTV providers, the useful question is not simply **“Who has the most channels?”**

It is:

**“Which service gives me the channels and viewing setup I actually need at a price I am comfortable paying?”**
